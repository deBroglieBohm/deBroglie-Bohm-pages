From the introduction

Quantum theory is extraordinarily successful, but its meaning remains difficult to picture. This book uses computer simulations to show one possible quantum world: a world in which waves evolve continuously and particles follow definite trajectories guided by those waves.

The videos are not intended as decorative illustrations. They are part of the argument. They show how interference, tunnelling and measurement can be understood as processes unfolding in time.

From the two-slit chapter

In the two-slit experiment, the particle is detected at one place on the screen. Repeating the experiment many times produces an interference pattern. In the de Broglie-Bohm theory, the particle has a definite path, but that path is guided by a wave passing through both slits.

The result is neither a classical particle picture nor a classical wave picture. It is a different kind of picture: a wave and a particle acting together.

From the delayed choice chapter

Wheeler's delayed choice experiment is often presented as if the present can alter the past. The de Broglie-Bohm account gives a more concrete description. The particle always has a definite position, while the wave evolves through the experimental arrangement that is actually present.
